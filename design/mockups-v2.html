4 · In-game — the board becomes a map

Five fixed bands, zero page scroll. At 29px per tile, names and prices come off the board: zone bands + ownership tint + a wedge + the owner’s token chip say everything at a glance, and the ticker names the tile you’re on. The one action that matters is always the big green button under your thumb — never below the fold.

5 · The deed sheet — where photos earn their bytes

Decision sheets can’t be swiped away; the coral bar counts the auction timer down. This is the one place photography appears — a self-hosted 14KB WebP of the actual city, loaded only when someone is deciding to spend money on it. First paint of the whole app: zero images.

Desktop in-game — rebalanced, same language

Desktop keeps three columns but the board centre is emptied of the feed, trivia and wordmark — it holds dice, whose turn, and the primary action only. The feed moves to the left rail; the pot and NEPA blackout become chips in the top bar.
